# Act III ("The Door"): Design

Date: 2026-11-02. Status: APPROVED by the user ("go for it", 2026-11-02).
What the user said is stated plainly. **[Claude's call]** marks decisions Claude made that the user can revisit.
**[Claude's guess]** marks story details Claude proposed in chat that the user did not object to but did not explicitly confirm.

Read with: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-act2-design.md (the whole-game story frame, THE SECRET, the four forces) and
docs/LORE_PITCH.md (every name and lore text already in the game).

## Intent

Act III pays off the whole game. Acts I and II built the Ashen Queen up as an ancient tyrant. Act III reveals the truth.

**The reveal (user):** "OMG I did not expect this", and "OHHHH, *that's* what that text was referring to." The reveal
should be **unassuming but astonishing**: the "power" everyone feared was **just a child all along**, and that should be jaw-dropping.

**The feeling after it (user):** **sympathy.** She is an **innocent, blameless child** who doesn't understand what is happening
or why she is in isolation. The anger belongs to the **people and forces controlling her.**

**The finale (user):** a **big, fun fight, one way or another.** Saving the child must not rob the players of a big-bad fight.
And it must be **cherished, not rushed**: no "one dialogue option and the game is over", no "two or three rounds and the game is
over". Even if the party wins fast, there must be **decent scenes and dialogue to close the story in an epic, cinematic,
feel-good way**, with **something that makes you ponder whether you did the right thing.**

## The truth (for the builder; the player learns it in Act III)

- **She is a little girl: the vessel and the victim (user, 2026-11-02; "kind of the Naruto treatment": an amazingly strong power
  sealed inside her).** This replaces the Act II spec's "she was a hatchling" (never shown in Act II, so nothing in the game changes).
  A thousand years ago the Crown of that age sealed far more of the world's fire than anything should hold inside a child, to
  break the Ember Pact and take its power. The fire is the "Ashen Queen" of the legend; the girl is only its vessel. The legend,
  the statues, the rhymes and the festival were the Crown's cover story. Inspiration is for the idea of a sealed power in an
  innocent vessel only; nothing is borrowed from that story.
- **[Claude's guess]** The endings follow from this: *end her* breaks the vessel and the fire takes the shape of the terror;
  *seal her* locks the vessel away again; *save her* draws the fire out of her, and the Crown tries to take it for itself (which
  gives the save path its big bad).
- **She is held in a giant lair under the capital (user)**, where the Crown has kept her prisoner **for generations (user).**
  **[Claude's guess]** The fire stopped her ageing: she has been a child for a thousand years, alone, mostly asleep.
- **[Claude's guess, from the Act II spec]** The blight and the shared dream are a frightened child reaching out in her sleep.
  The "knocking" on the door in the dream is her. The Ashsworn heard a voice and answered.
- **[Claude's guess]** The Crown still draws on her fire today. The capital's lamps and hearths are the only ones in the realm
  that never went cold during the blight; the Crown calls it the King's blessing.
- **Geography (user approved, 2026-11-02: "a good hybrid").** Acts I–II say the dream door is "under the Wyrmback"
  and that the twelve Great Ones lie in a ring as the Wyrmback Mountains. To fit both: **the capital is built inside the ring of
  the Wyrmback, where the mountains close around a high valley.** The undercity goes down through the mountain's roots, so the
  door is under the capital *and* under the Wyrmback. The Great Ones really did lie down around her. The Crown then built its city
  on top of the prison, to own it. The thirteenth bell (existing lore) is for her.
- **[Claude's call]** The current king or queen does know: the throne passes the secret down. Most of the Crown (soldiers, envoys,
  Marsh) sincerely believe the legend. This keeps the people grey and the throne guilty.

## Act III shape (user)

**"Start in the capital, then find her, then return to the capital, and when questioned, that's when the branches break and we
throw hands with whatever side wants the smoke."**

### 1. The capital (the opening)
- The party's first visit to the big city (it was kept out of Acts I–II on purpose). Grand, bright and warm while the rest of
  the realm went grey. Full of Crown propaganda: statues of the Queen's defeat, parades, rhymes.
- **[Claude's guess]** The big explorable place of Act III, in the spirit of the Academy grounds: districts with landmarks,
  side quests, shops, nobles, back alleys, secrets (player tastes: GTA5/Skyrim/Zelda; varied, explorable places).
- **[Claude's guess]** Small things are off if you look: the warm lamps, a sealed royal crypt, workers who never come up from
  the foundations, records with the same gaps as the Academy's. The party follows the last clues down.

### 2. The lair under the capital (the middle)
- **A giant lair under the capital (user):** foundations, old vaults, royal crypts, then something much older.
  **[Claude's guess]** A real dungeon with fights and puzzles, built by the Crown to hold something terrifying: wards, chains,
  the iron of the Iron Ring (Act II lore), guards who've never been told what they guard.
- **The meeting (user's reveal, Claude's staging guess):** at the bottom, the door from the dream. Beyond it, not a boss arena
  but something quiet: a small space, a child's things, and a little girl who is scared of *you*. No boss music.
  She doesn't know she is "the Ashen Queen". That name is a story other people tell about her.
- **The "OHHHH" (user):** Act II text the players already read now means something else. **[Claude's call]** Right after
  the meeting, the lore codex marks every legend entry the meeting reframes ("Read again") with a short new line underneath.
  That way the click lands even 15 hours later. Examples: the shared dream (the knocking was her), "Hold... the door" (the
  Warden was keeping a child in, not a monster back), the thirteenth bell, the Iron Ring ("someone wanted something to stay
  here"), the altered records. Every lock, ward and seal held a kid in.
- **[Claude's guess]** The party's dragon, scared of her fire all of Act II, sees her and is the first to go to her.

### 3. Back to the capital (the turn and the finale)
- The party comes back up. **Questioned (user):** the Crown, the party's own side and the other forces demand to know what was
  found and what will be done. **[Claude's guess]** A big scene in the throne room or the great square, with every force
  present, real dialogue, and the dragon reacting.
- **That's where the branches break (user):** the party chooses one of the three endings below. Then **everyone who wants the
  smoke comes for them (user)**, and the finale fight breaks out in and around the capital.
  **[Claude's guess]** The city explored in the opening becomes the battlefield: streets you walked are now where you fight.
- **The truth about the Crown comes out on every path [Claude's guess].** The Crown is the common enemy unless the party
  chooses to end her.

## The three endings (user)

**Only these three (user, 2026-11-02).** The older LORE_PITCH ideas (renew the Pact, fly away, claim the throne, the Stardew
ending) are dropped as separate endings. Each must feel different and each must have a big finale fight.

1. **End her.** The violent choice. **She transforms into a terror (user): showing what people have been afraid of.** The Ashen
   Queen of the legend, huge and burning. The biggest boss fight of the game. The world is safe. **[Claude's guess]** It is a
   little hollow: the epilogue doesn't let you forget who she was.
2. **Seal her.** **Sad and sombre (user).** **[Claude's guess]** Ashby's way: she goes back into the dark, forever this time,
   possibly not understanding why the people who were kind to her are doing it. The finale fight is against whoever tries to
   stop the sealing or take her for themselves (the Crown wants her power, not her gone).
3. **Save her.** **Rewarding (user).** It must not skip the finale (user). **[Claude's guess]** The people who need her to stay
   a monster come for her: the Crown, and whoever else profits. The finale's big bad is the Crown's true power, a royal figure who
   has pulled the strings all along, or something they unleash. The party fights *for* her.
   **[Claude's guess]** It is the hardest ending to earn, drawing on the clues, the lore and the bonds built over the game.
   **[Claude's call]** The gate is generous, never a hidden fail state: if the party is short, the game makes clear what is missing
   before the choice, so nobody locks themselves out of the ending by accident.

- **Any ending is still choosable at the turn, including betraying the side you chose (Act II spec).** Betrayal has consequences.
- **The game never says which ending was right (Act II spec).** Each epilogue carries a cost to ponder (user: "something that
  makes you ponder if you did the right thing").

## The four forces in Act III

- **If you sided with the Crown (user): you rebuke the Crown and wage war for honour.** **[Claude's guess]** It cuts both ways:
  some Crown soldiers you knew from the Redoubt defect and join you, others become enemies, and Marsh has to choose too.
  Only the "end her" path keeps the Crown fighting beside you, and even then the truth is out.
- **[Claude's guess] Academy:** Ashby sincerely believes in the seal and pushes for "seal her". On the save path he must be
  convinced or he stands in the way. He never was a villain.
- **[Claude's guess] Fae Courts:** bargains and balance, not right and wrong. They may want her power, or want her free because
  the Crown broke the old Pact. Wild cards. The `fae_clause` favour (the dragon owes the Courts one) is called in here.
- **[Claude's guess] Wyrmwatch:** dragon hunters who believe the legend and want her dead: the loudest voice for "end her".
  If you sided with them, you have to turn their hunt around.
- **[Claude's call]** Who stands with you and who fights you at the finale is decided by your Act II side, the hooks recorded in
  Act II (`banner_<force>`, `fae_clause`, `wyvern_egg_saved`, `wyrmwatch_belled`, `marsh_asks_ashby`, `lantern_eyes`) and the
  ending you choose. Each force's reaction to each ending is a small table the plan writes out.

## The dragon, grown

- **Grown in Act III,** big enough to fly.
- **Power (user):** just slightly below the party's power level. The user's example: if a fighter rolls 4d10 for damage, the
  dragon rolls about 2–3d10. Enough that "I have a dragon on my side", never carrying a fight.
  **[Claude's call]** A rules test checks, at levels 11–13, that the dragon's average damage per round stays at about 50–75% of
  the party's average martial damage per round.
- **Flying is special, not travel (user).** Since only one person can fly, it is one **cinematic, climactic moment** in the finale,
  not a way to get around the map. **[Claude's guess]** The dragon takes off over the capital during the final battle (to reach
  the big bad, break a siege or rescue her) and the rider gets a flying sequence: breath, a dive, then the landing into the boss
  fight. Exact form is left to the builder; it must feel big.
- **The rider prompt (user, must have):** the user will give the flight to their fiancée. When the moment comes, the game pauses
  and asks **"Who rides {dragon}?"** with a choice for each player. If the wrong player triggered it, they can back off and hand it
  over in one press. Nothing starts until the rider is confirmed. **[Claude's call]** Both players see the prompt; either can pick;
  the chosen rider confirms. Solo, it goes straight to the one player.
- **[Claude's guess]** This bookends Act I: the egg hatching was the fiancée's treat, and she gets to fly it.

## The finale must not feel rushed (user)

**[Claude's call]** These rules make "cherish the moments" concrete:
- **Several beats, not one fight.** The questioning scene → the break → a fight through the capital (more than one stage) →
  the flight → the boss fight in phases → the aftermath. The boss has story beats at phase changes (dialogue, scene changes)
  that happen however fast the party deals damage. A perfect party still sees every scene.
- **Real dialogue at the turn.** The choice is a conversation with every force present, with real dice rolls where they fit,
  not a single button.
- **A real ending sequence.** Scenes after the fight: her (each ending's version), the party's dragon, each companion and the
  faction leaders saying their piece, then Thistledown and the people from Act I (Brannoc, Elowen, the kobolds, Buoy).
  Then an epilogue in slides showing how the world turned out from the player's choices, ending on a line that leaves you
  wondering if it was right. The narrator stays warm and wry (Mercer + Mulligan).
- **Credits** after the epilogue.

## Goals (Act III, levels 11–13, about 8 hours)

1. **The capital** as the big new place to explore, with landmarks, side quests and secrets, warm while the realm went grey.
2. **The lair under the capital:** a real dungeon down to the door.
3. **The reveal**, staged quietly, plus the "Read again" codex reframes.
4. **The turn:** the questioning and the three-way choice.
5. **Three endings**, each with its own finale fight and the full ending sequence.
6. **The four forces' reactions** and who fights where, from Act II state.
7. **The grown dragon:** balanced just below the party, plus the one cinematic flight with the rider prompt.
8. **Levels 11–13 for all 12 classes (user OK'd the range)** following SRD 5.2: class and subclass features, 6th–7th level spells,
   ASIs/feats. The level cap rises from 10 to 13. XP rules unchanged.
9. **The Oathbound Ideals: [Claude's call]** the Third Ideal ties into her and the endings, lightly (a hidden inscription in the
   lair and one boon, like the Second). No full Oath system.
10. **Buoy [Claude's call]:** stays pettable in Thistledown, comes to the capital with the Thistledown folk at the end, and is in
    the ending sequence.
11. Co-op stays first-class.
12. **Carry-over [Claude's call]:** an Act II save that beat the Warden continues into Act III. A "Start at Act III" option makes
    level-11 characters with starter gear and default story flags.

## Non-goals

- Flying as overworld travel.
- An open-ended post-game. **[Claude's call]** The game ends after the credits; the save is kept.
- Online multiplayer, new species.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- **A lame or too-quick finale (user):** one dialogue option and it's over, or two or three rounds and it's over.
- **A save-her path with no big fight (user).**
- A reveal that is loud or telegraphed early. It must be unassuming (user). Act II's secret rules still hold for anything a
  player can see before the meeting: the capital's opening may grow suspicion about the Crown, but **nothing before the door
  hints that she is a child.** The secret guard test extends to Act III text before the meeting.
- A villainous child, or one who is to blame for anything.
- An ending that the game declares "right".
- An overpowered dragon, or a dragon flight that only one player can ever get (the rider prompt must make the hand-off easy).
- Grimdark: still bright fantasy, PG-13. Sad and sombre is allowed (the seal); horror and gore are not. Her terror form is
  frightening the way a dragon is, not gory.
- Grid-like or empty maps.

## Constraints (unchanged)

- Unity, C#; rules engine SRD 5.2, engine-free (tools/RulesTests). Dice always visible.
- Data-driven content (WorldDb, DialogueDb, QuestDb, MonstersDb, LoreDb); art through ArtRegistry ids; Ninja Adventure art plus
  Claude-authored gaps.
- Every phase ends with rules tests + play-mode tests green, screenshots reviewed, exe + zip rebuilt; merge to main and push after
  the full suite passes.
- Working names are approved in bulk; list new ones in LORE_PITCH.md.

## Left to the builder

Capital districts and their quests, the lair's layout and puzzles, the child's name and exact look (a little girl who reads as
"just a kid" in the existing art style, with the sealed fire showing only faintly, if at all), the reigning monarch and the save path's big
bad, boss stat blocks and phases (SRD 5.2 spirit, using the Act II legendary/lair/phase engine), the flight sequence's mechanics,
the force-reaction table, the epilogue slides, XP pacing to land level 13 near the turn, and the build order (phases with their
own plans, like Acts I–II; levels 11–13 first).

## Cut order if scope runs over [Claude's call]

Untouchable: the reveal and its staging, the three endings each with a big fight, the unrushed ending sequence, the rider
prompt and flight, the capital and the lair, levels 11–13.
Cut first, in order: (1) the number of capital side quests; (2) the Third Ideal; (3) per-force variety in the epilogue slides;
(4) the size of the fight through the streets (fewer stages).
