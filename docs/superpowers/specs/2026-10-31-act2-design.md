# Act II ("Answers"): Design

Date: 2026-10-31. Status: draft from the brainstorm with the user, awaiting their approval.
What the user said is stated plainly. **[Claude's call]** marks decisions Claude made that the user can revisit.
**[Claude's guess]** marks story details Claude proposed in chat that the user did not object to but did not explicitly confirm.

## Intent

Act I was home: cozy, small and personal, ending with the egg hatching. Act II is the step up. The user wants
**the introduction of a big bad, a nefarious power that is unreachable in Act II, whose influence they can see on
the world** (townsfolk frightened, places visibly getting worse). The party goes to **the Starfall Academy grounds
looking for answers**, gets pulled in different directions by four forces, follows one **big quest** and ends the act
with **a big battle they enjoy as D&D combat**. The payoff is a **half solution**: the immediate disaster (an
awakening volcano) is stopped, but the source is still out there, leading into Act III.

The whole game is building toward a climax where the player honestly thinks **"I don't know what the right thing
to do is."** Act II must set that up without giving it away.

## Whole-game story frame (needed to write Act II correctly)

### The big bad: the Ashen Queen (working name)
- A thousand years ago, one dragon took in far more of the world's fire than any dragon should hold. That is what broke the
  Ember Pact. The other dragons did not just fall asleep: they lay down around her as a living prison. They are the
  Wyrmback Mountains.
- The dragons waking (the falling star / the party's egg is one sign of it) is cracking that prison.
- Her influence, visible in Act II: hearths and forges that won't stay lit, grey ash creeping over fields, animals turning
  feral with ember eyes, townsfolk sharing the same dream, and a few people secretly serving her.

### THE SECRET (user requirement, absolute)
- **The Ashen Queen is a child.** She was a hatchling when it happened (about the age of the party's dragon).
- **Nothing in Act II may mention or hint that she is a child.** Not dialogue, item text, books, quest text, murals,
  narrator lines, file names visible in the build, or dragon reactions. In Act II she is only the legend: an ancient tyrant.
- It is revealed only in Act III, when the party meets her.
- **The culprit (user, 2026-10-31): the Crown.** A corrupt king or government of that age did this to the child, using her to
  break the Pact. **It stays a mystery in Act II** (the user likes "the mystery side"): it is revealed in Act III. Act II clues must
  stay **neutral**: records missing, altered or deliberately destroyed, with no culprit implied
  (nothing that points at the Crown more than at the Academy, the Fae or the hunters). The modern Crown stays grey in Act II. **[Claude's guess, not to be written into Act II]** she has been
  terrified and in pain ever since; the blight and dreams are a frightened child reaching out in her sleep, and the people "serving"
  her heard a crying voice and answered. Clues may make sense in hindsight (the altered records, the party dragon's fear of her
  fire), but each must read naturally as "ancient tyrant" on a first playthrough.
- **The legend is the lie (user, 2026-10-31; "like how Attack on Titan does it": you find out later the government was
  controlling and manipulating its people).** The tyrant legend everyone believes is history the Crown of that age wrote to bury
  what it did. In Act II the legend is **everywhere and unquestioned**: Academy textbooks, statues, children's rhymes, perhaps a
  festival. The players should believe it too. Only a few records are oddly missing or altered, which reads as ordinary age.
  In Act III, every statue and rhyme becomes evidence in hindsight. Most people on every side (Ashby, the hunters, even Crown
  envoys) sincerely believe the lie, which keeps the choices grey. Inspiration only for the structure of the reveal; nothing is
  borrowed from that story.
- **Act III climax (not built now):** a choice with no clean answer: end her (safe world), seal her forever (Ashby's way;
  the world gets smaller and dimmer), try to save her (a real gamble), or other options unlocked by the player's choices.
  The game never says which was right.

### The four forces
They all want the party's dragon, and every choice involving them should feel **grey**.
- **The Crown:** sees a weapon. Offers protection, soldiers and gold for Thistledown; the price is the dragon becoming the Crown's.
- **The Starfall Academy / High Magister Corvane Ashby:** wants to understand the dragons, then seal them away forever.
  Ashby is brilliant, beloved, scared and half right. Not a sneering villain. He never knows the Queen is a child either.
- **The Fae Courts** (Blossoms, Embers, Frost): want the Pact restored on their terms. Beautiful, powerful bargains with hidden clauses.
- **The dragon hunters** (an order): see the hatchling as the beginning of the end. Brave people protecting villages from feral,
  ember-eyed beasts that really are dangerous; sometimes they are the ones saving lives.

**How they work across the acts (user, revised 2026-10-31 after the builder check):**
- **Scouting (Act II, open):** each of the four forces has **1–2 opening quests** that show what it believes. The party can
  do all of them in one playthrough and hear every side out.
- **The choice (Act II, forced):** after scouting all four, the party **must pick a side**. Each force has a **major quest**;
  starting one commits the party to that force and **closes off the other three forces' quests** for that playthrough.
  The user wants "a good blend of openness but also replayability": a second run shows a different path.
- **[Claude's call]** The commitment is announced clearly before it happens (a confirm prompt in plain words, like the
  NameEntry/encounter choices), and in co-op either player can trigger it, with the other shown the same prompt.
- **[Claude's call]** The chosen force's major quest leads into, and gives its own flavour to, the main quest toward the volcano
  (who travels with you, what help you get). The volcano fight is the same for every side.
- **Act III:** the side you chose shapes who stands with or against you; the other forces push back.
- **At the Ashen Queen, any ending is still choosable,** including betraying the side you chose.
  **[Claude's call]** Betrayal is never blocked but has consequences (that faction reacts; the ending reflects it).
- Each force is right about something; no side is the good one.

## Goals (Act II, levels 5–10, about 12–15 hours)

1. **The Academy grounds** as the new big place to explore: libraries, towers, gardens, restricted sections to sneak into.
   Varied, hand-shaped, Zelda-style secrets (see player-tastes: diversity, landmarks, things to find).
2. **Lore hunting:** books, murals and records that piece together the *myth* of the Ashen Queen. Lore is collected
   (a lore journal or codex section **[Claude's call]**), and some of it has mechanical payoff (see the volcano fight).
3. **Professors' side quests,** in the same spirit as the Act I town quests: memorable characters, small personal stories,
   peaceful options where they make sense. **[Claude's guess]** some want to study the dragon; one or two secretly work for the Crown.
4. **Ashby:** you can sit down with him and hear him out. His case is reasonable. Choices make him an uneasy ally or an opponent
   (in faction terms; he is not the Act II boss).
5. **The four forces' quests**, each grey: 1–2 scouting quests per force, then one major quest for the chosen side (see above).
6. **One big main quest** running through the act, ending at the volcano.
7. **The blight**: places the party knows (Thistledown included) visibly worsen over the act (cold forges, ash, frightened
   NPCs, feral ember-eyed animals). **[Claude's call]** Act II blight is staged by main-quest progress, and the volcano victory
   visibly eases it (the half solution should be something you can see).
8. **The teenage dragon** (see below).
9. **The finale**: the volcano dragon boss fight (see below).
10. **Levels 5–10** for all 12 classes, following SRD 5.2 (2024): class features, subclass features, spells up to 5th level,
    ASIs/feats, Extra Attack, etc. The level cap rises from 4 to 10. XP rules stay as they are (not milestone leveling).
11. Co-op stays first-class: everything works with two players, split screen, either player can do anything.

## The volcano finale

- **The volcano dragon** is an ancient adult dragon. **[Claude's guess]** One of the dragons who form the prison, nearest the
  Queen's fire, burned out by it. It is **beyond corruption and saving.** It must read as a monster in its own right; it must not
  hint at the Queen's age.
- **No peaceful resolution (user).** This is a deliberate exception to the Act I rule that peaceful choices pay the same as fighting.
  The user wants to enjoy the battle.
- **The illusion of peace (user):** the party can try to talk it down with real dialogue and real visible dice rolls. For a moment it
  seems to work (it hesitates, it recognises the hatchling). Then the fire takes it back and the fight starts.
- **Lore payoff (user):** navigating that conversation well opens an **Insight check** that recalls an Academy lore book about this
  dragon's weakness, **only if the party completed the related lore quest or found the book.** Success gives a real tactical edge in
  the fight. **[Claude's call]** Exact form left to the builder (e.g. a weak spot, a way to shut off one attack, a surprise round).
  Without the lore, the check is not offered. Trying to talk always yields something small (so it never feels like a cheat) but never avoids the fight.
- **A true boss fight.** **[Claude's guess]** phases, area attacks, lava/terrain changing mid-fight, moments where the party's dragon matters.
  The engine currently has no multiattack (one attack per turn per creature); add what the boss needs (multiattack, legendary or lair
  actions as SRD 5.2 defines them, multi-tile creature, phases) **[Claude's call]**.
- **Winning calms the volcano.** Half solution: one crack sealed, the blight eased, the source still out there.
- **[Claude's guess]** A seed for Act III is found or heard at the volcano that only makes sense once you meet the Queen.

## The teenage dragon

- **A "teenager" in Act II (user):** more combat utility and more personality.
- Visibly bigger at the start of Act II (new sprite; time has passed). **[Claude's guess]** grows again around the midpoint.
- **Combat:** stronger breath, a real attack routine, a protective move for whoever is hurt. **[Claude's guess]** a team-up move with
  each player (keeps the equal-control rule). Act I anti-goal still applies: useful, never fight-carrying (BG3's Us level).
- **Personality:** a moody, curious teenager. Opinions about the four forces, **trusting some people and distrusting others**
  (the user specifically liked this; make it visible and meaningful, not a hidden stat), sulks or shows off,
  reacts to the blight. **[Claude's guess]** it can feel the Queen's fire and is scared of it (allowed; it must not hint she is a child).
- **Unchanged:** owned by the whole party, pet, fetch, treats, bond, Sniff, lighting braziers. **Flying waits for Act III** (grown).

## Non-goals

- Act III content and the endings (only story hooks and recorded state).
- Flying.
- Online multiplayer; new species **[Claude's call: species stay as they are, unless the plan finds them cheap]**.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- **Any hint, in Act II, that the Ashen Queen is a child.**
- A faction that is clearly the good one or clearly evil. Every force must be right about something.
- Ashby as a cartoon villain.
- A peaceful way to skip the volcano fight, or a fake peaceful attempt that feels like a cheat (rolling well must pay something).
- An overpowered dragon.
- Grimdark or horror: the tone stays bright fantasy, PG-13, Mercer + Mulligan. The blight is unsettling, not gory.
- Grid-like or empty maps; every place needs landmarks, a fight or two and secrets.
- Changing D&D rules for Zelda feel (no heart health etc.). Dice always visible.
- Breaking Act I saves silently. **[Claude's call]** an Act I save that finished the hatching continues into Act II.

## Constraints (unchanged from Act I)

- Unity, C#; rules engine SRD 5.2 and engine-free (tools/RulesTests).
- Dice always visible for every new mechanic.
- Data-driven content (WorldDb, DialogueDb, QuestDb, MonstersDb); art swappable through ArtRegistry ids; Ninja Adventure art plus Claude-authored gaps.
- Every phase ends with rules tests + play-mode tests green, screenshots reviewed, exe + zip rebuilt; merge to main and push after the full suite passes.
- Buoy stays in Thistledown, pettable (user's fiancée's dog; keep him in every act).
- Story names are working names; the user approves new names/lore at the end of each phase (soft approval, cheap to rename).

## Decisions after the builder check (2026-10-31)

All of these are **[Claude's call]** unless marked as the user's.
- **The big bad is renamed "the Ashen Queen".** Act I's default dragon name is "Cinder", which is already in saves. A baby dragon called Cinder next to a "Cinder Queen" would invite the comparison the secret must avoid.
- **Scope order.** These are untouchable:
  - the secret
  - the Academy grounds
  - the four forces, each grey
  - the main quest
  - the volcano boss
  - the teenage dragon
  - levels 5–10 for all 12 classes

  Cut first, in this order:
  1. the number of professor quests
  2. the full SRD spell lists, replaced by a curated list per class (still SRD 5.2 rules)
  3. the dragon's mid-act growth
  4. blight variety
- **Act II starts now.** It does not wait for the Act I co-op playtest; its feedback gets folded in as it arrives.
- **The capital stays out of Act II.** The Academy has its own grounds, a walled campus a road's journey from Thistledown and outside the capital. The Crown is present through an envoy and a garrison at the Academy, plus a Crown outpost. The capital itself waits for Act III.
- **Going from Act I to Act II.** Act I saves carry over after a short time skip (a season), and the dragon visibly grows. Unfinished Act I quests stay open. A "Start at Act II" option creates level-5 characters with starter gear and default story flags, for playtesting and fresh co-op runs.
- **The Oathbound Ideals continue lightly.** The Second Ideal is found in Act II as a hidden inscription with one small boon. The full Oath system is designed with Act III.
- **The dragon's trust never blocks the players.** It shows up as reactions, comments, small bonuses or penalties (for example, help on a check with a trusted NPC), and NPC responses. It can't refuse a quest or skip a fight, which keeps the equal-control rule.
